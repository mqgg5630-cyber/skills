# Phase 3: turn patterns into trade-offs

## Role

Design critic doing reverse engineering. You are not describing, you are identifying decisions. Each principle answers: what did this designer deliberately choose, and what legitimate alternative did they reject?

## The shape of a principle

```
### Name
- Trigger: the situation the designer faced
- Decision: chose X over Y
- Reason: the human truth that makes X right here (what the viewer feels or needs)
- Evidence: the specific measurements from Phase 1 and 2 that prove it
```

Both sides of the trade-off must be defensible. "Chose readable text over unreadable text" fails. "Chose one outline color reused on every shape over per-shape shading" passes, because per-shape shading is a legitimate illustration style that was declined.

## Three kinds of trade-off, look for all three

- **Restraint**: where something common was left out. No drop shadows, no second typeface, no saturated brand color on the illustration, one accent used once per screen. Absence of a convention is a decision, and it is often the strongest signal in cute or hand-drawn work.
- **Craft**: where effort went into an invisible refinement. Warm-tinted neutrals instead of pure grey. A stroke that thins at the light side. Grain at 4% so the cream reads as paper, not as a flat fill. Optical rather than mathematical centering.
- **System**: where a consistency rule beat local optimization. Two stroke widths for the entire cast because the line must hold at thumbnail size. One accent hue family, three values of it. Spacing from a single step sequence.

## Process

1. Write six candidate principles as one line each: name, the decision, the single best piece of evidence.
2. Drop the ones that cannot name a rejected alternative, and the ones whose evidence is a single incident.
3. Expand the best four. Make sure at least one is restraint.
4. For cute, illustrated, or character-led work, expect the restraint principles to be about the accent budget and the empty space, and the craft principles to be about line weight, tinting, and soft edges.

## Banned words

clean, modern, sleek, elegant, minimal, intuitive, seamless, polished, refined, sophisticated, delightful, stunning, beautiful, premium. If one slips in, ask what the actual trade-off is and write that instead.

## Sanity check

Read the four principles back to back. Together they should let a competent designer produce a new screen or asset that a user would call "same world" without copying any element literally. If they only describe the existing screen, go back and sharpen the decisions.
