# Phase 4: apply the taste to something new

## Build against the Design Map

Set the tokens first, then compose. Do not eyeball a palette, copy the hex values. Do not invent a spacing step that is not in the measured sequence.

Order that keeps drift low:

1. Ground, then the warm or cool wash over it.
2. Texture at its measured strength. If the reference grain is 4%, your grain is 4%.
3. The primary subject, sized by the density map: how much of the frame the reference actually fills, not how much feels right on a large monitor.
4. Secondary marks (accents, sparkles, hairlines), counted, not sprinkled. If the accent budget is one warm hue, spend it once.
5. Type last, aligned to the same optical axis as the subject.

## Verify, do not rationalize

- Re-measure the output: sample the ground color, the ink, the accent, and the stroke width. Diff against the Design Map.
- Check contrast where text will sit on top of the design. A background that will host chat bubbles, labels, or UI chrome needs its quiet zone to survive real content; measure the quiet zone, do not assume it.
- Ask of each of the four principles: is it visible in the output? If a principle cannot be seen, it was not applied.
- Look at the output at the size it will actually be seen. Thumbnail first, then 100%. A stroke that disappears at thumbnail size was too thin.

## Common failure modes

- **Token drift**: the palette is right but the values are approximations. Sample the actual pixels.
- **Density inflation**: filling the frame because empty space feels like unfinished work. The reference's emptiness is a decision.
- **Accent creep**: adding a second and third accent because the composition feels flat. Fix the composition instead.
- **Mechanical symmetry**: centering everything when the reference deliberately off-centers to create tension or to leave room for content.
- **Texture on top of text**: grain applied after type, softening the letterforms. Apply texture to the ground, keep type crisp.
- **Copying elements, not decisions**: reproducing the exact sparkle of the reference in a place where it means nothing. Use the principle, not the sticker.

## Report

Deliver the artifact plus a short note: the four principles in one line each, and the places where the new context forced a deviation from the reference. Deviations stated openly are part of the taste; silent ones are drift.
